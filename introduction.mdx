Yantra Commons is an open-source hardware nonprofit based in Bengaluru, India, built around a single conviction: the devices you own should be fully understandable, repairable, and improvable by you. Too much modern hardware is locked behind proprietary designs, opaque firmware, and deliberately scarce spare parts — engineered to fail on a schedule and to resist repair. Yantra Commons pushes back by publishing everything: schematics, PCB layouts, bills of materials, firmware source code, 3D-printable enclosures, and step-by-step build guides. Every project is free to use, modify, and share.

## Why Open Hardware?

Most consumer hardware treats its own internals as a trade secret. Schematics are never published. Firmware is binary-only. Replacement parts are sold exclusively through the manufacturer — or discontinued entirely after a few years. When a device breaks, your only option is to buy a new one.

<Note>
  Closed hardware doesn't just inconvenience you — it has real environmental and economic costs. Devices that can't be repaired end up in landfills. Components that could be reused for decades are thrown away because a single proprietary part is unavailable. Open hardware breaks this cycle by making every design element accessible, so anyone can repair, reuse, and improve what they build.
</Note>

## What's in Every Project

Every Yantra Commons project ships with the complete set of files you need to go from nothing to a working device — and to understand every decision along the way:

- **Complete schematics** — full circuit diagrams in open formats, so you can trace every connection
- **PCB layout files** — Gerber files and editable source files (KiCad) ready to send to any PCB fabricator
- **Bill of materials (BOM)** — a structured parts list with part numbers, quantities, and suggested suppliers
- **Firmware source code** — fully commented, buildable from source, with no binary blobs
- **3D-printable enclosure files** — STL and source files for enclosures designed to fit the exact PCB
- **Step-by-step build guide** — assembly instructions written for the skill level each project targets

## Who Is This For?

Yantra Commons projects are written for anyone who wants to understand, build, and own their hardware — not just use it. That includes:

- **Makers and hobbyists** who want to build working devices from scratch and learn how they actually function
- **Engineers and designers** who want a reference implementation, a starting point for a custom design, or a fully open platform to build on
- **Repair enthusiasts** who are tired of being locked out of devices they already own and want schematics and parts to fix them
- **Students and educators** who need real, complete hardware projects to teach electronics, embedded systems, or PCB design
- **Anyone** who believes that understanding your own devices is a basic right, and that hardware should serve its owner — not the other way around

You don't need to be an expert to get started. Each project lists a difficulty level and the tools required, so you can find something that matches where you are right now.
