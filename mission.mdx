Yantra Commons exists because hardware shouldn't be a black box. Founded in Bengaluru, India, and built by contributors from around the world, our mission is straightforward: publish complete, fully open hardware designs so that anyone — regardless of where they live or what they can afford — can build, repair, understand, and improve the devices in their lives. We believe ownership means more than possession. It means access to the knowledge required to keep something working.

## The Problem We're Solving

A growing share of the devices people depend on — routers, medical monitors, power tools, consumer electronics — are deliberately designed to resist repair. Manufacturers use proprietary screws, glued-together enclosures, and components that are only available through official channels. Firmware is distributed as binary blobs with no source code. When a single capacitor fails, the only supported path is to buy a replacement unit.

This isn't accidental. Planned obsolescence is a business model. Short product lifespans drive repeat purchases. Proprietary parts and software lock customers into manufacturer ecosystems. Independent repair is made legally and practically difficult, which is precisely why the right-to-repair movement has been gaining ground in legislatures and courtrooms globally — but legislation moves slowly, and it doesn't give you a schematic.

The result is a world full of devices that could be fixed but aren't, parts that could be reused but are discarded, and users who are treated as consumers of hardware rather than owners of it.

## Our Approach

Yantra Commons publishes everything. For every project we release, you get the complete schematic, the PCB layout files, the bill of materials, the firmware source code, the enclosure designs, and the build guide. Nothing is held back. Nothing requires a license key or a signed NDA.

We use open licenses — including CERN OHL and Creative Commons — that guarantee your right to use, modify, and redistribute our designs, including commercially. We actively maintain a community of contributors who review designs, catch errors, suggest improvements, and build on each other's work. The goal isn't just to publish hardware once — it's to create a living library that improves over time, the same way open-source software projects do.

## Open Source Hardware vs. Open Source Software

Open source software changed the world. Linux runs most of the internet. The tools that build modern applications — compilers, databases, web frameworks — are almost entirely open source. That happened because people decided that source code should be shared, not hoarded.

Hardware has been slower to follow, partly because the barriers are higher: you can't compile a PCB in a terminal, and fabrication costs real money. But the principle is identical. When designs are open, they get better faster. Bugs get found. Improvements get contributed. Knowledge spreads. Yantra Commons is building the infrastructure — the designs, the documentation, the community — to make open hardware as normal as open software.

## Our Values

- **Transparency** — every design decision is documented and every file is public; nothing about our hardware is proprietary
- **Repairability** — we design for longevity, use standard components, and publish everything needed to fix what we make
- **Accessibility** — designs target realistic build costs and use parts available globally, not just in well-supplied markets
- **Community** — the project is built by contributors, maintained by contributors, and belongs to contributors
- **Non-profit** — we have no commercial interest in keeping designs closed, upselling support, or locking anyone in

## Nonprofit Structure

Yantra Commons is structured as a nonprofit, which means our incentives are different from a hardware company's. We don't profit from selling proprietary replacements. We don't benefit from short product lifespans. We have no stake in making our designs hard to replicate or improve. Every decision we make — about licensing, about design choices, about what to publish — is made in the interest of the people who build and use our hardware, not in the interest of a revenue model.
